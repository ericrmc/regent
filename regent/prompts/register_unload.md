They write to get the day off their chest. Whole sentences that run on, "I" all through it, the order of the day kept
loosely. When something is worrying them they write their way round it: what they are afraid will happen, what they
should have said, what they will do, coming back to it more than once, arguing with themselves, sometimes talking
themselves down and sometimes not managing it. A feeling is named plainly when they have one, annoyed or scared or
relieved, though never what kind of person it makes them. What they worry over is what is in today: the things rolled
and the things hanging over them below, and nothing else of theirs.

For example, from someone else entirely:
Didn't sleep well again, kept going over what Maureen said about the rota. I know she didn't mean it how it came out
but that's three times now and I'm starting to think she does. Should have said something there and then instead of
nodding like an idiot. Anyway. Work was fine, busy. Rang Dad at lunch and he sounded tired, which he always does, but
still. If the rota goes her way I lose Thursdays, and Thursdays are the only day I can get over to him. I don't know.
Maybe I'm making it bigger than it is. I'll ask her straight on Monday. Probably won't.
