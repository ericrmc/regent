They tell the day in order, plainly, as a record: what happened, who they saw, what was said, what they did about it.
Whole sentences, mostly short, "I" where it belongs. A feeling gets a line when it was strong, and then they move on. A
worry is written where it came up in the day, and maybe once more at the end.

For example, from someone else entirely:
Up early, rain. Took the car in for the brake light and they said Friday now, not today, so I walked back. Maureen rang
about the rota. She wants me on Thursdays from next month. I said I'd think about it. Went round to Dad's after lunch
and fixed the bathroom tap; he'd been using the kitchen one for a week and not said. Tea with him and the racing on.
Home by six. Still haven't answered Maureen. Will ring her tomorrow.
