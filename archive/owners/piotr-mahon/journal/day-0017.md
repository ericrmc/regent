# 2019-03-20

Window open. Didn't smell cold. Blackbird in the sycamore. Bosun on shins, Wafer on the sill with her back to me, tail moving slowly, looking at the bird in the way that means nothing will come of it.

Pilsudski on the third stair. Carried the dog.

Kitchen warm before I lit anything. Tea, thumb on the chip. Toast with plum jam, and the last of the lemon drizzle—ate it standing up.

Penhale at half ten, Weston Mill. Big turnout. Man of eighty-eight, sons from Bristol and Reading in the car park with their coats over their arms, red in the face. Coffin pale oak with his cap on the lid, flat tweed. I took the left front corner. Six of us, slow step on the gravel. Smell of cut grass. A wasp going round the wreath on the lid. One of the sons watched it the whole way in and didn't brush it off. I liked him for that.

Marta did the committal. Voice low and even. Hadn't sweated in a suit since September. I was counting the minutes by the end.

Afterwards I went round the back and undid my tie and stood in the sun against the warm brick. Haven't leaned on anything warm for months.

Market by the church on the hill. Fish stall—bed of wet ice. Woman in a green fleece sluicing the boards. Daffodils with heads still tight in zinc buckets. Fried onions—smell came at you first. Bought a hot dog. Stood with the paper translucent in my hand.

Bicycle at the veg man's barrow. Black, basket on front, dynamo lamp. Tyre soft. Put my thumb on it—gave like dough. Veg man said it was his nephew's, been like that since Tuesday, he'd gone off with the keys. I said I had a pump in the car. Knelt on the cobbles in my funeral trousers. Tyre took the air. Then I heard another hiss, faint, from the stem—steady thin sound like a kettle. "It's the valve or the tube." "It'll see him to the bank."

Second-hand books table. Two men. "The bird is flown at a weight, and the weight is the control, because a bird that is not hungry does not return."

Man with a box of screws. Read what was left of the number on my wrist. Came out with a belt in a paper sleeve. "That'll be six pound. Thursdays and Saturdays Torpoint."

Afternoon: hearse, cloths, wheel arches. Warm in the yard.

Half five, put Lorraine's plate on the front step of number 14, under a saucer. "Thank you, very good, P."

Barry at the bottom of his garden with the hedge trimmer. "Friday, Piotr. Definitely Friday." White smear on the shed window but the sun was in the glass.

Belt fell out of my jacket pocket. Held it, running it through my fingers. Black loop with a ribbed inside, warm from me.

Took the back off the washer with the screwdriver, torch in my teeth. Cats in the doorway. Old belt hanging in two pieces, dry and split, like a thing that had died in its sleep.
