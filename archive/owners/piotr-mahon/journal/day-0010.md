# 2019-03-13

Fog against the glass like a wet sheet. Foghorn slow. Turn my head on the pillow and it goes thin and one-sided.

Cupboard open since Christmas—bottom hinge pulled out of chipboard, hangs at an angle. Beer mat wedging it. Every night I shut it, every morning it swings out an inch.

Wednesday the 13th. Yesterday was the 12th—Nula's. Every year of my working life I send her a card with a robin on it. Yesterday I went round the barber's, the thermos, the hospital envelope, eggs. Never thought of it once.

Van wouldn't start. Damp in it. Yard at seven, Denny with the chamois.

Rang her. Rang seven, eight times. Click, fumbling, the chair. "You didn't forget. You remembered late. A person who forgets doesn't ring up in the yard with the fog on his voice."

She got two cards—girl does her feet, man at church, Alan's with a printed message. On the sill next to the cactus. Line went mid-call. Call ended. Letters gone under the crack.

Removal at ten—man eighty-something on a bed with hospital rail. Shoes by the door, black, polished, tissue in the toes. Somebody had been going to take him out.

Rang about the thermos liner at 12:30 from the stairwell. Cream wall, light on and off above. Showing as delivered, left in a safe place. Five working days for a query.

Home 5:50. Nothing on the step. Looked under the wheelie bin.

Barry at the gate. Slippers. Cardigan. "That box. Yours has the card." He'd seen the thermos on my back seat. The dent. Cataract in the left.

"I've got a Stanley in the garage. Old green one. Pint and a half. Never took it anywhere. Maureen used to say one picnic and didn't care for it."

Back in four minutes under his arm like a loaf. Heavier. Dent of its own.

Two thermoses on the draining board now. They looked like a pair in a shop.
