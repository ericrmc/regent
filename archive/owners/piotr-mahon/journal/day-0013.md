# 2019-03-16

Cold you could cut bread with. Window clear, frost on the fence. Hedge black against shell-coloured sky.

Cupboard door still in the hall against the wall. Cupboard standing open on its tins like a mouth left open in sleep. Beans, tomatoes, three pink fish, pears Mum liked. Cup with teaspoon standing on the sill for no reason.

Breath visible by the cooker.

Seven o'clock—drill next door. Three short, one long. Goes through me in the plaster. Cups shook on the rack.

Reg rang at 10 past. Loud, formal. Up at five, foot down out of bed and it told him no. "Knee's the size of a swede." Doctor says rest it. Says wait for me, next Saturday. Before I answered: "Is that a drill?" "Tell him it's a bad time of year."

Yesterday's plan kicked to today. Today's kicked to next week.

Washing machine man: Tuesday not Monday. Part on a van somewhere between Exeter and here. 8-12 Tuesday. Wrote on calendar above the pig—WASHER, TUES, 8-12. Lot of writing for a man who says he isn't busy.

Did the compost. Frozen cat tracks. Fine stuff went under the cooker. Lay on my good side, cheek on the floor. Everything there: beer mat, two pennies, bottle top, dog biscuit gone grey. Arm to the shoulder. Smell of old kitchen, chip fat, dust. Put the beer mat in the bin, took it out again—good mat, Courage, ship on it. Stood it next to the cup of compost on the sill.

Window box from the back step, frozen, dead geranium like a hand. Broke the top with a spoon. Scooped the black compost, spread it on the frost, pressed with my thumb. Nothing going in it. Far too early. Hands under cold tap till they hurt. Drill went again.

Eleven o'clock I went round with the door.

Barry came out sleeveless, sawdust in his arm hair. Drill in his fist pointed at the ground like forgotten. "Piotr." Said it right, near enough.

"Chipboard. Forty years and then it's cake. Want a dowel and some glue? Got some in the shed."

Looked at what he'd been drilling. Side gate. Taken the old bolt off, fitting new one, black, hasp. Holes neat and clean, pale dust below each. Not the fence. Thought I'd known. Thought I didn't.

Back with dowel in paper bag, yellow glue, good kind, half used. "Push it in wet, snap off flush, leave overnight. Don't screw till tomorrow, won't hold." Keep the tube. Picked up the drill.

At the gate before I thought to say something. About the hedge. About the letter, been on the doorstep since August. Turned round. He was back at the bolt with his ear near it, listening. One thumb on the hasp. Drill went again.

Hinge at the kitchen table. Door upside down, light coming across. Smell of yellow glue and bread, frost on my sleeve. Dowel in with the screwdriver handle, small wet sound. Sat with my hands either side like keeping something warm. Bosun's chin on my foot. Corner of the letter visible from the hall. Cupboard still open.
