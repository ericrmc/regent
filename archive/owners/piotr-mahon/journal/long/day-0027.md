# 2019-03-30

Twenty to six, and the window over the sink was white. Not grey, not the running grey of Thursday. White, as if someone had hung a sheet on the outside of the glass. I couldn't see Barry's fence or the path or the top of the pear. Tea, thumb on the chip. The cupboard under the sink was open its finger's width and the beer mat was on the lino, and this morning I didn't pick it up. I stood with my foot next to it and drank the tea and let the cupboard breathe its bleach at me.

Bosun would not go out. He stood at the back door with his nose to the gap and his whole body leaning away from what was on the other side, and I said, "It's only fog," and he looked up at me as if I'd said only the sea. I opened the door a foot. The cold came in wet, and a smell of cut grass that has no business in March, and every sound was near and flat. A car door somewhere. A gull, that I couldn't see, saying the same thing over and over. Pilsudski came down off the third stair to watch and sat on the mat with his back to the dog, which is how he insults him.

The card was on the hall shelf. I'd put it there Thursday night, and it had sat under the Derriford envelope for two days, a proper one from the newsagent's, a blue one with a boat on it, and inside I had written nothing. Roisin was fifty-five yesterday. The twenty-ninth. I know it as I know my own postcode and I had it on the back of my hand on Wednesday, and then the vet, and the rain, and the clipboard, and it wasn't there. I stood in the hall with the card in my fingers and could feel the day go by behind me, the whole of yesterday, with her in it, in Stoke, in her kitchen, waiting for the phone or not waiting.

I took the phone out of my jacket. It's got a crack across the bottom corner from a fortnight ago when it went off the arm of the chair, a little spider of glass that catches my thumb. I keyed her number. It rang five times. Then her voice said, "Hello?" and behind it a man's voice said something and a tap was running, and she said, "Piotr?" the way you say it when you are turning away from a room. I said, "Roisin, I'm a day late." She said, "Late for what?" and I heard her hear it herself, and she said, "Oh. Oh, love." And the tap stopped, and I heard the other voice ask her something, low, an ordinary question, was it the– and she said, "Hang on, it's just Piotr." Just. I said I wouldn't keep her. She said, "No, it's–" and there was a noise like a hand across the mouthpiece, and then the line was full of nothing, that shut-in nothing, and then the beeping. I don't know which of us did it. I stood there with the phone against my ear. I did not ring back. I put the card in my jacket, with the boat pressed against the lining, and thought I would post it and know what I meant by that, too.

The parcel was meant to be the seed potatoes. Fifteen pounds of Charlotte and Pink Fir Apple from a man in Devon, the box promised for Friday by noon, and it had not come. There was a little card on the mat when I got back last night that I had not looked at properly, and it said SORRY WE MISSED YOU, with a tick in a box that said no one was in. I was in. I was in from six. Nobody knocked. I turned it over this morning and there was a depot, a phone number, and a line saying redelivery could be booked online, and I put it on top of the fridge with the rest. It is Saturday. The tubers will be sitting in a sorting office, going soft in the dark, sprouting for nobody.

I went to the plot anyway. I walked, and I could have told you by sound alone where the Plain was and where Peverell Park Road, because the fog took the buildings out and left the noises. A shutter going up. Someone's spade on a step. A woman said good morning to me from about a yard away out of a white nothing and I said it back and never saw her. On the Plymouth road I took the bus, and sat at the back, and the driver had the lights on and the wipers going for a rain that wasn't falling, only standing in the air. There were six of us. We all looked straight ahead.

The gate at Ridgeway sticks unless you lift it, and lifting it in the wet is like lifting a dead man's arm. The track was stones and standing water. I couldn't see past the third plot. The shed on thirty-one came up out of the white like a boat, with its bootlace, and I stood with my hand on the wet felt and did not go in.

"You've no spuds in," said Reg, from somewhere on my left. He was on his bucket, at the edge of his own bed, in the big waxed jacket, with a flask in his lap and a knitted hat. I hadn't heard him. He never comes in by the gate, I think he sleeps in there. I said they hadn't come. "They never do," he said, "not when you want them. Sit down, boy, you look like I've had you on a rope." He shoved the flask at me. It was tea with sugar in it and something else, brandy or a memory of brandy, and I drank it and coughed, and it went through me like a hot wire down to the belt. I said I didn't drink Saturdays before twelve. "That's medicine," he said. I drank again.

He was talking about his bees. I did not know he had bees. Two hives at his daughter's, over Yelverton way, that he goes and sits by. He said a hive that loses its queen doesn't stop and sit down, it makes new queens, a whole row of them at once, six or eight or ten, in the cells at the edge of the comb. "And then the first one out goes round and stings the others in their beds," he said, "or the workers do it for her, I forget. Kills the lot of them, the ones that haven't hatched. All that work, all that royal jelly. Spent it all before they knew which one was going to be any good." He wiped the mouth of the flask with his cuff. "Waste, you'd say. But they can't know. So they do the lot." Somewhere in the fog a wood pigeon began, four notes and a stop. Reg screwed the top on. "Are you giving him a good look, that lad of yours, or shall I say the potatoes, are you going to do the potatoes?" I said what lad. He said, "I've got you muddled," and laughed, and I did not ask him again.

I did the leeks. There was nothing else. Half a row of them, which had stood since October, and I lifted them with the fork and the earth came away black and cold and stinking sweetly, and I knocked the roots on my boot, and laid them on the sink. I stood a good while with a leek in each hand. The white went on. It seemed to come up out of the ground rather than down.

When I came back at one there was a note through the door. Not a card. A page from a pad, folded twice, and in a hand I knew, the round one of the woman at the deli: Piotr, I saw you go past Thursday in the wet. Didn't shout. Jar's behind the counter, it's the last of the batch, come for it tomorrow if you can't today. I read it in the passage with my coat on. She had never written me a note. I have bought the jam for eleven years, and I did not know she knew where I lived. It was a fair walk, in that, for a woman with her hands in a bowl. I turned it over. On the back, in smaller writing, she'd added: Nobody should have toast with nothing on. I stood there and I found I could not tell whether I was going to laugh.

The kitchen was too bright, after. I put the leeks in the sink and ran the tap, and the cupboard door had swung right out, wide open for the first time, showing the pipe and the pan of old cloths and the bucket, everything, its little private dark. I looked at it. I meant to shut it. I got as far as the beer mat, and knelt down for it, and my knee went off like a shot, and I stayed there on the lino with my palm flat next to the mat, and the phone in my pocket pressing into my hip, and the corner of it, the crack, digging in. Bosun came and stood at my shoulder and breathed.
