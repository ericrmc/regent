# 2019-03-23

Twenty to six. Curtain gap—line of white across the blanket and Bosun's back. Cold, clear, frost on the inside of the glass. Wafer on the pillow next to my ear, purring in her sleep like a small engine. Rain gone and left all that sharpness behind.

Pilsudski on the third stair. Bosun in my arms, warm and stiff and heavy, eyes on the cat over my shoulder.

Tea, thumb on the chip. Toast with what was left of the jam. Scraped the glass with the knife, smear the size of a fifty pence piece.

Bag of compost on the kitchen floor between the cooker and the cupboard. Bent to lift it. Seam went. All the dark wet weight came out across the lino in one slide, over my slippers and into the dog's water dish. Stood with half a bag in my arms. Got the dustpan and coal shovel. Scooped it handful at a time into a bucket. Slug in it. Put the slug out on the step. Compost on lino—stays damp, gets into the pattern. Dark line in the carpet I'll find in June.

Cupboard door behind me hung open, quarter inch off the frame. From down there on the floor you see it truest. Bottom corner stands proud. No amount of turning the little screws brings it in.

Ten to seven. Noise next door. Hammer drill first, then a whine that rose and fell. Saw out. On a Saturday, ten to seven. Came through the party wall and the window. Bosun got up and came and stood against my calf. Wafer went over the top of the fridge and out of sight. Finished the floor with a wet cloth. Barry said a word to himself I heard as clearly as if he'd been at my elbow.

Half seven. Phone rang. Roisin. "Are you up? I knew you'd be up." Said gutters—don't come. Sister coming over with kids, madhouse, don't want me up a ladder. Said it was no trouble. "Next week, or the week after. It's not going anywhere." Radio or kettle behind her. She took a breath. Then she rang off.

Whole morning now with nowhere to put it.

Made a second tea. Took the split bag, folded the top, stood it in a bucket. Kept it together. Boot of the Octavia: fork, hessian sack, sprouted seed potatoes in egg boxes from the back bedroom window since February, pale stubby shoots going purple at the tips.

Phone again as I was locking the door. Girl from vet. "Mr Mahon? So sorry. Wednesday, ten past four?" I said I'd have to see about Wednesday. "Ten past four. Ring us if you can't." Didn't write it down. Said it twice going down the path. Felt it slipping.

Ridgeway site at nine. Road up there bright and stony with frost in the ruts where sun didn't reach. Gate with its usual squeal. Flat glittering plots, netting stiff, every cane with a white cap. Pigeons on the shed roof. Radio somewhere, low. Reg at the tap in his flat cap and long brown coat, filling a can.

"You're early. Ground's like iron." I said I'd brought compost. He looked in. "That's not compost, that's peat and hope. What did you give for it?" Shook his head.

Fork in with my foot. Each forkful came up in a slab. Cold in the knuckles through the glove. Where sun got it, top inch went dark and soft, smell came up—iron and wet stone. Dug a trench, length of six paces. Put compost in. Sprouted potatoes on the bench outside the shed in their egg boxes to harden. Didn't plant. Too early. Reg would have told me.

Sat on the upturned bucket by the shed with tea from the flask. Sun had got into the plot. Face warm, hands cold. Wood pigeon on the water butt. Reg talking to another man about drains. "You can't hurry a wet spring, you can only wait for it to be a dry one."

Thought about Wednesday. Ten past four. Whether Marta would let me go. How the day would have to be arranged. Hand on phone in pocket. Sat there. Sun moved along shed roof by about the width of a plank.
