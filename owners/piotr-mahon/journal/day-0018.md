# 2019-03-21

Ten past three and ten to five and by twenty to six I'd been awake so long the rain stopped being noise and turned into the shape of the room. Came in about two—gutter at the back first, then the whole roof. Bosun pressed along my shins, felt him not sleeping either. Wafer gone. Pilsudski on the third stair with his eyes open.

Before I left I did what I'd promised myself last night with the belt half round the pulley and my knees gone. Put the washer on empty, short cycle, stood in front of it with my arms folded. Drum turned. One thump at the spin, then settled. Didn't touch it. Turned off the tap at the wall.

Rain flat and grey and total, coming off the roofs in sheets. Mutley Plain emptied as if a whistle had gone. Bus with its lights on and nothing in it. Newspaper in the gutter turning to porridge. Deli awning pouring off its edge in a rope. Woman inside with her hands flat on the counter. Raised two fingers off the wheel. She raised her chin. Nothing said.

Removal form under the stapler. Pink-and-white sheet. Ackland lady from Tuesday. Hadn't filled it in. Sticky note in square capitals: it couldn't wait.

Cold in the office. Wrote the times. Hospice, ward side, my name. Carried it like a stone in my shoe.

Stairwell cold and wet-smelling. Green rubber treads dark with boots. Light doing its flicker—three quick and a long.

Saw the bracket. Lowest stanchion of the handrail by the fire door, worked loose. Gap between plate and wall—finger wide. One screw out on the tread. Brass one, slot mangled by somebody's bad screwdriver forty years ago.

Biscuit tin on the shelf under the fire notice—Huntley & Palmers Christmas one. Full of screws, brass hooks, cup washers, odd keys. Sat on the third step and tipped a handful out.

Radio below, Radio Devon, door propped with a bucket. Man talking about the river—the Tavy or the Plym, some catchment. Lag between the rain and the peak flow tells you about the ground and not the rain.

Found one. Number ten. Held the bracket to the wall, screwdriver in the slot, turned. It bit. Put my weight on the rail and it held.

Sit with the mess of screws on the step. Thought I ought to sort them. Never will.

Noon. Sausage roll on the middle landing. Tin still there. Marta stepped over them. "Good clear-out." Said the rail was done now.

Rang the vet. Said Mahon about Bosun's check Monday, had a funeral at half ten, could I move it. Line went.

Afternoon thin. Dennis Pym rang about a rota. Found something to do at the far end of the garage.

Home by five. Bosun on the hall carpet. Washer—little water in the rubber lip, nothing else. Small load—two shirts, a towel. Spin—no thump at all.

Eggs on toast, sitting down. Gas fire ticking. Rain thinned to fine drift. Nobody in the street.

Barry half seven. Stood on the step under the porch light with the long yellow level in both hands like he'd been asked to carry it into a church. "There. Found it. It was in the shed all along." Took it. Cold, slightly damp. Bubble in the little window gone as green as before. White paint smear near the end, shape of a thumb. My landing ceiling. Neither of us looked at it.

Took the level through to the kitchen. Laid it flat along the top edge of the cupboard door I'd hung. Bent to watch the bubble. Slid toward the left end and hung there. Held my breath. Saw I was leaning on the counter with my whole weight and the counter wasn't true either. Straightened. The bubble ran back the other way. Didn't know which of us had moved.
