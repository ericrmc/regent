# 2019-03-28

Ten past three. Rain sideways, loud on the back bedroom. Bosun under the bed. No thunder.

Half past four, gave up. Pilsudski on the third stair, ears flat, awake. Looked at the front door. Bosun pressed to my calf.

Tea. Toast with nothing on it - the jar's a smear.

Window running so hard I couldn't see Barry's fence. Garden a grey sheet.

Walked in. Big black umbrella from the hall - the one for the family at the graveside.

Gutters running like a weir. Empty road. Bus going by, lights on, lower deck vacant. Carrier bag went past at my speed. Woman at the deli with her hands in a bowl, not looking out.

Yard was a lake. Gulley ankle deep with water.

Rang the vet at nine from the middle landing. Under the light. Cream paint cold through my shirt. Stairwell smelling of wet wool and drains. Monday nearly gone from the wet. Rang six times. Machine picked up. Woman's voice, calm: "You're through to the surgery. Please hold, your call is important to us." Music. Flute. Stood with the receiver against my good ear. Alan's feet crossed the office floor over my head. A door. Line went. Not a click. Just the flute, then nothing. Dead receiver. Didn't ring back.

Marta came down with the clipboard. "Sorry. Alan wants everyone's done by Friday. Insurance. Staff ones." Voisey & Daughter along the top. Under it: for the purposes of the firm's records only. Name. Address. Postcode I knew as my own teeth. Next of Kin. Wrote Roisin Keogh. Stoke address. Relationship. Pen made a small blot. Partner was true eleven years. Went on to - Any Medical Conditions We Should Know Of - wrote none. Gave it back with Relationship blank. She looked at it. Looked at me. "I'll leave it for you," she said. Took it up. Biro on its string knocked the rail like a tongue against a tooth.

Half nine. Rain came down properly. Office. Marta and I stood at the window. Two minutes. All that water. The gulley gave up. The yard one grey pane. Mercedes up to their hubcaps.

"That's the ten o'clock cancelled for the flowers."

Family came anyway. Eleven under one umbrella. Sister held it and wouldn't let go. Water off the ribs down my collar. Dennis not there. Kieran and me and two chapel staff. Light coffin for a heavy day. Hymn almost lost. Watched the sister's shoes filling with water. Didn't look down once.

Bottom step under an inch. Took my socks off in the passage - nobody sees.

Kieran shaking like a dog. "That's the wettest I've ever been. Fell in the Cattewater once." Pleased with it. Jacket on the radiator, steaming. Looked at the handrail bracket swinging. Hanging off since Christmas. "That's coming off." Fair fault.

Went to look for screws. Cupboard under the bottom flight. Peek Freans tin - Highland cow going brown. Nula's tin. Heavy. Doesn't rattle - slides like shingle. Brass slotted, blued, black japanned no one uses now. Screw-eyes. Cup hook. Chrome caps. Cabin hook. Brass shoehorn the size of a finger, with a hole at the end.

Kieran held the torch. Found one at last - long, brass, a bit dull. Turned it in until the bracket stood firm on its own. Put my full weight on the rail. It took it. Kieran tried too, swung his whole body like a child on a gate. "Yeah, that's good."

Afternoon - laying out. Woman from Mannamead, eighty-three, good face. Rain on the skylight. Little radio on the shelf - Nula's - tape across the dial. Woman talking about rivers. "...what you're measuring is the lag, between the rainfall and the peak flow, and that tells you about the ground and not the rain." Did her hair. Brown shoes with a strap, worn at the outer edge of the heel. Thought - she walked on the outside of her feet.

Left at five. Street wet and shining and empty. Bin blown over. Gap in the cloud above the Hoe with yellow light coming through. Went home.

Cold, smelled of dog. Two pierogi, ruskie, fried in butter. Ate them with a fork and sour cream from the tub.
