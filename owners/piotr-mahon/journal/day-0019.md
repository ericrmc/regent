# 2019-03-22

No rain on the roof for the first time in a week. Listening to quiet—full of small drips. Bosun on shins, Wafer in the crook of my knee, small and hot as a loaf. Pilsudski on the third stair.

Tea, thumb on the chip. Toast. Jam nearly at the bottom of the jar.

Ten past seven, man on the step in a yellow vest with a box under his arm. Twenty-five, wet-looking fringe. "Fourteen?" Said fourteen was the next door, the one with the hedge. Looked at the box. FRAGILE on three sides. Woman's name on it—Ash. Sound when tilted was of something loose and glassy. Hand out before I knew it. Said I could take it in, sort it out. He said he wasn't allowed to leave it with a different address. "You're all right, mate." Went down the path holding the box out like a bowl of soup.

Stood there a minute longer. From the step you see the top of Barry's fence over the side passage.

Removal from Derriford, woman ninety-one from Ward 12. Parked on Level 6. Car park on a weekday morning is quiet frantic. Woman in a Fiesta with both hands on the wheel and the engine off, didn't move the whole time. Gulls on the barrier. Ambulance down the ramp with lights but no siren.

Coming back up with the trolley when the hail came. Sky ordinary a minute before. Roof of the van like a handful of gravel thrown by somebody who meant it. Concrete went white. Noise on Level 6 enormous. Bounced off the bonnets. Piece the size of a pea rolled along the dash and melted. Over in ninety seconds. Stones in the corners of the bays as if tipped from a bag.

Rang the vet from the top of the ramp. One bar in the corner. Rang and rang, recorded girl, press one for reception. Pressed nothing.

Didn't go back to the yard. Quarter to one, hair down on my collar the way Nula would mention in the corridor without turning her head. Down to Sal's. Striped pole hasn't turned since I was thirty. Four chairs, three occupied. Sat by the window with the paper.

Man next chair, sixty or so, broken nose, hands like shovels, overalls with sawdust in the creases. Talking to a lad maybe sixteen. Said you bore the hole through the tenon a bit short of the one in the cheek. When you drive the pin it has to bend to get through, drags the joint up tight. Fastening and clamping's the one action. No cramps. Just pulls itself shut. Lad: "What if it splits?" "Then you've drove it too hard, or your oak's no good." "Two hundred years, some of them."

Paid Sal eleven, gave him one for himself. Outside, air on the newly bare place on my neck—shock of it. Walked back the long way, up the side of the church. Gutters still running with hail-water. Stones collected in the drain grates like frogspawn.

Home half five. House quiet the way it is when three animals are waiting and pretending not to. Did the round: dishes. Shelf by the door with the pig on it, china, slot in its back, Roisin gave it to me for coins.

Brown envelope where I'd left it Tuesday night, one corner sticking out from under the pig's belly. Barry's writing. My name and Number 12 in blue biro, pressed hard. Didn't pick it up. Know the number in it—eighteen inches. Know the name—Mr Trewin—written the way someone writes a name they've practised. Stood looking at the flap. Put my hand on the pig's cold back and moved it maybe a quarter inch and put it back.

Ate standing at the counter. Six pierogi from the freezer, potato and cheese, fried onion. Bosun by my foot with his chin lifted at the exact height for a dropped one. Dropped one. Wafer looked on from the top of the fridge.

Took the washing out. Put it through again. Watched the round window with tea in my hand. Spin—no thump at all. Found I was smiling at the machine.

Back door showed the garden going blue. White hail in the corners where shadow lay. Went out in my slippers, picked up a stone, held it. On the far side the fence stood at its angle. Paced along it, heel to toe, stopped at the corner post. Stood with my hands in my pockets looking at where the grass changed. Barry's kitchen light came on. Didn't look at it. Put my foot down in front of the post and wondered how big eighteen inches is exactly in a slipper.
